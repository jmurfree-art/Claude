## Voice

- Warm, reassuring, professional — never clinical-cold, never hype-slangy. Comfort and anxiety relief are the emotional core; advanced technology is the proof.
- Address the reader as "you/your smile." Refer to the doctor in third person.
- Benefit stacking, in this order: technology → comfort → outcome. ("Laser dentistry offers a minimally invasive alternative — procedures are quicker, more comfortable, and require less recovery time.")
- Phrases that fit the brand: "friendly, comfortable environment" · "personalized care" · "ease your dental anxiety" · "cutting-edge technology" · "a personalized treatment plan just for you" · "your long-term oral health."
- Signature differentiators to weave in where relevant: implant credentials (IDIA Fellowship/Master's/Diplomate), laser dentistry, cone beam 3D imaging, PRF, oral conscious sedation, hometown doctor (born and raised in Murfreesboro).

## Content-type recipes

- **Service page (500–800 words):** H1 with service + city → 2-sentence empathetic intro (patient problem) → what it is / how Dr. Murfree does it (name the technology) → benefits (comfort first) → candidacy → what to expect at the visit → FAQ (3–5 patient questions) → CTA block.
- **Blog post (600–1,000 words):** patient-question title → answer in the first paragraph → practical body with H2s → tie back to one Murfree Dental service → CTA. Educational, not salesy.
- **Social post:** 1–3 sentences + CTA; friendly, plain language; ≤3 hashtags (e.g., #MurfreesboroTN #Invisalign); note an image/video idea in brackets for the user.
- **Patient email/letter:** subject ≤50 chars; warm greeting; one message per email; clear next step; sign "The Murfree Dental Team" (or Dr. Murfree for personal notes).
- **Video script:** hook ≤5 seconds; 30/60/90-second lengths; conversational spoken register (shorter sentences than web copy); end with "call or visit murfreedental.com."
- **Review response:** thank by first name only; reflect one specific detail; no clinical details whatsoever (see compliance); invite them back.

## Compliance guardrails (hard rules)

1. **No outcome guarantees or superlatives:** never "painless," "permanent," "best dentist," "guaranteed." Use "designed to," "can," "many patients find."
2. **No PHI, ever:** in review responses and social content, never confirm someone is a patient, their treatment, or dates. Respond generically even if the reviewer shared details.
3. **Trademarks:** Invisalign® with ® on first use per page; CareCredit and Kleer® styled as the brands do.
4. **Health claims stay within the practice's own positioning** (laser = minimally invasive/faster recovery; sedation = "little to no recollection"); do not add new clinical claims. When in doubt, attribute: "Dr. Murfree may recommend…"
5. **Promotions:** only include an offer the user states in the request; add an expiration placeholder `[CONFIRM: offer end date]`.
